Bog oak · Bog ash · Carbon-dated

Wood that waited millennia under water

These trees were never cut down. They fell into lakes and bogs long ago, and the water kept them. Our divers bring them back, and our workshop in Lithuania turns them into floors and interiors.

Descend

The rarest wood on earth is found, not felled.

Bog wood is the most sustainable timber we know: no living tree is touched. Every trunk is located by hand, lifted, dated and slowly dried before a single board is made. Below is that journey, step by step.

I
Long before usLakes & peat bogs

Fallen

An oak falls into a lake and sinks into the silt. Without oxygen it does not rot. Over centuries, tannins in the wood react with minerals in the water and the timber slowly turns from gold to deep brown and black.

II
DiscoveryDeep water

The dive

For more than twenty years our team has dived the deepest lakes, cleaning them and searching for fallen trunks. Each piece is chosen by hand for its beauty and character.

III · The lift

Back into the light

The trunk is raised, still heavy with water, and sees the sky again.

IV
AuthenticationRadiocarbon

Dated

A sample from the trunk is sent for carbon dating. The result tells us when the tree was alive, and it is the age you will be standing on.

V
SawmillThe reveal

First cut

Only when the trunk is opened do we see its colour. No two logs are alike: some are honey-brown, some nearly black. The water decided, not us.

VI
PatienceControlled drying

Drying

Wood that has lived under water leaves it saturated. It is dried slowly and under control, because rushing this step cracks it. This is where most of the time goes.

VII
WorkshopLithuania

The hands

In our workshop artisans select, plane, stain and finish every board. Bog oak is made into multilayer boards that stay stable with changes in temperature and humidity and work with underfloor heating.

VIII
InstallationAnywhere in the world

Fitted

Our own craftsmen fly in to install the wood wherever it is going, from a private residence to a yacht. The team that made it is the team that lays it.

The end of the journey

And then, one morning, it is simply your floor.

Holds its colour

Bog oak resists discolouring from sunlight and wear from daily use.

Stays stable

Multilayer boards do not swell with changes in temperature or humidity.

Warm underfoot

Compatible with underfloor heating systems.
