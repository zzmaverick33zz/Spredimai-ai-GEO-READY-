A lead time of centuries, even millennia.

Bog trees were never cut down. They fell into lakes and bogs long ago, and the water kept them. Every trunk is located by hand, lifted, dated and slowly dried before a single board is made.

  1. An oak falls into the waterLong before us, trees fell into lakes and peat bogs and sank into the silt.
  2. Sealed from oxygenWithout oxygen the wood does not rot. Over centuries, tannins react with minerals and the timber turns from gold to deep brown and black.
  3. Found and raisedOur divers have searched the deepest lakes for more than twenty years. Each piece is chosen by hand.
  4. DatedA sample from the trunk is carbon-dated. The result tells us when the tree was alive.
  5. Slow dryingWood that has lived under water comes out saturated. It is dried slowly and under control, because rushing this step cracks it.
  6. Crafted and installedMade into multilayer boards in our workshop in Lithuania, and laid by our own craftsmen.
