01 Origin

A lead time of centuries, even millennia.

Bog trees were never cut down. They fell into lakes and bogs long ago, and the water kept them. Every trunk is located by hand, lifted, dated and slowly dried before a single board is made.

  1. 1

    An oak falls into the water

    Long before us, trees fell into lakes and peat bogs and sank into the silt.

  2. 2

    Sealed from oxygen

    Without oxygen the wood does not rot. Over centuries, tannins react with minerals and the timber turns from gold to deep brown and black.

  3. 3

    Found and raised

    Our divers have searched the deepest lakes for more than twenty years. Each piece is chosen by hand.

  4. 4

    Dated

    A sample from the trunk is carbon-dated. The result tells us when the tree was alive.

  5. 5

    Slow drying

    Wood that has lived under water comes out saturated. It is dried slowly and under control, because rushing this step cracks it.

  6. 6

    Crafted and installed

    Made into multilayer boards in our workshop in Lithuania, and laid by our own craftsmen.

04 Material

Coloured by time, not by stain.

Long years under water and peat leave their mark on a trunk: its colour, its age, its character. Every board carries a record of that. Here is what we can read from it.

  1. Tannins meet mineralsIn oxygen-free water, tannins in the wood react with minerals and slowly darken it.
  2. No two logs are alikeSome are honey-brown, some nearly black. The water decided, not us.
  3. Raised, not felledThe trees were never cut down. Each trunk is located by hand and lifted.
  4. DatedA sample from the trunk is carbon-dated, so the age is known, not guessed.
